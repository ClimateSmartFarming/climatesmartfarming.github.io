Dairy Climate Adaptation and Mitigation Fellow **Julia Olmstead** worked to improve her farm's manure management and silage storage during the Dairy Climate Adaptation and Mitigation Fellowship (Dairy CAMF). Julia is a co-owner and operator of Mid-Knight Dairy, LLC, a 300-acre dairy farm in Jamestown, New York. Her dairy milks 140 Holstein cows and raises 130 heifers, with a focus on milk production and quality. In recent years, Mid-Knight Dairy has been utilizing technology such as robotics to achieve that goal. The farm also produces haylage and corn silage using time, money, and soil-saving techniques such as no-till corn planting.

Julia's advisor, Tim Terry, recommended that she participate in the fellowship program. Before joining, her farm was constructing a manure storage facility. Erosion has long been a concern in manure management — a problem becoming increasingly significant with the growing frequency of damaging storms. Julia hoped that Dairy CAMF would teach her how to prepare her farm for future storms.

## Planning That Pays Off

As Julia created her climate resiliency goals, she decided that her projects needed to make sense both financially and for climate adaptation and mitigation. One project she implemented since the program began has achieved both. The construction of a modernized manure storage pit allowed her farm to save approximately **$20,000 in fertilizer in the first year alone**.

In the future, Julia hopes to eliminate her farm's use of ag-bags to store silage, noting that they are wasteful both in terms of plastic and in forage lost to spoilage. To achieve this, she plans to complete the construction of bunk silos.

> "We've got half of it done, but we still need to pour more cement and get some walls." — Julia Olmstead

Julia has calculated that the completed silos will result in a **$15,000 increase in net income per year**, while also allowing for more consistent forage quality that will increase milk production at Mid-Knight Dairy.

## The Value of Sitting Down and Planning

As she reflected on Dairy CAMF, Julia concluded that the best part of the fellowship was simply being able to sit down and plan for the farm's resilience.

> "I think as farmers, we get busy doing the day-to-day tasks, and we often don't sit down and think about the future." — Julia Olmstead

Gaining a better understanding of what her farm needs to succeed against future threats — and the role of budgeting in making it happen — was invaluable. Julia also stressed the importance of policy support for farmers pursuing climate adaptation.

> "I think keeping politicians and the public aware of this is crucial." — Julia Olmstead

Without the assistance of government grants, she believes future climate-related projects will be challenging to attain given the low-profit-margin nature of agriculture.
