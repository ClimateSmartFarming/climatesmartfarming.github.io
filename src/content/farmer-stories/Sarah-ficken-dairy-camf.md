**Sarah Ficken** works in the dairy industry both as a farmer and as program manager for a dairy training program in New York. She is also on the leadership team for the Dairy Climate Adaptation and Mitigation Fellowship (Dairy CAMF). Even as a member of the Dairy CAMF leadership team, Sarah still learned a great deal about how to prepare her farm for extreme weather events.

Sarah and her husband, Chris, run a first-generation farm, New Moon Farmstead, in Munnsville, NY. Their farm is highly diversified, featuring dairy, beef, pigs, chickens, and additional livestock. Since 2023, Sarah and Chris have primarily focused on beef rather than milk production, but hope to resume milk production in the spring of 2026.

## Joining Dairy CAMF

Sarah was drawn to the program because of her previous work with Dairy CAMF leader Allison Chatrchyan. Sarah and Allison first met at Empire Farm Days in 2016. Still new to farming at the time, Sarah was interested in Allison's extension program, Climate Smart Farming. Having graduated from Cornell University with a degree in Natural Resources, Sarah said her work with Allison was a perfect match. When the opportunity arose to join Dairy CAMF leadership, she jumped at the chance.

> "Whenever Allison has an opportunity, it's a good one, so I say yes." — Sarah Ficken

## A Farm Full of Climate Challenges

Every year brings a new challenge to the Fickens' farm.

> "There is probably an entire book of climate change challenges on our farm." — Sarah Ficken

Both Sarah and Chris frequently utilize the lessons taught during Dairy CAMF to benefit their own farming operations. One of the major stressors the farm experiences is water — some years bring too much and others bring too little. In 2025, water has been their most significant challenge.

> "We've just been clobbered with water." — Sarah Ficken

That year's storms brought so much water that even the strongest drainage systems struggled to handle it. Much of their adaptation efforts have focused on preventing erosion. In 2016, they adopted no-till practices and haven't looked back — even during massive rainfall events, they have mostly avoided erosion in their fields.

## Managing Heat and New Pest Pressures

Temperature fluctuations have been another challenge. Heat stress and cold snaps have challenged their animals' health and comfort, while new pest pressures have also reduced the farm's profits. To manage heat, their barns are designed to cool livestock passively, utilizing natural ventilation to reduce reliance on electric-powered fans. The farm has also recently switched most of its lights to LEDs to reduce electricity costs.

## The Value of Shared Knowledge

> "Hearing different viewpoints helped us synthesize more information." — Chris Ficken

Both Sarah and Chris noted that hearing the perspectives of different dairy farmers through Dairy CAMF was invaluable, allowing them to learn far more than they would have been able to learn alone.
