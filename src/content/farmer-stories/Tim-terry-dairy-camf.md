As an advisor in the Dairy Climate Adaptation and Mitigation Fellowship (CAMF), **Tim Terry** worked with dairy farmer Julia Olmstead as she reduced risk on her farm. As the Farmstead Strategic Planning Specialist with Cornell's PRO-DAIRY, Tim combines his background in dairy management with his engineering expertise to help dairy farmers design sustainable and productive facilities.

Tim decided to participate in Dairy CAMF as a professional development opportunity. His goal was to enhance his ability to design dairy facilities that benefit both the environment and the farm. He also wanted to hear about the best practices and technologies available for dairy management, so he could better advise farmers on how to plan and reduce risk and costs.

## Efficiency as a Climate Strategy

One of the primary objectives for farmers in the battle against climate change is increasing on-farm efficiency. Tim ensures that his structural designs are as energy-efficient as possible.

> "There's no sense spending extra diesel or gas to get a job done when we can do it differently and save time, effort, and money." — Tim Terry

Improving climate resiliency on dairy farms is another core mission. Runoff from rainwater has long been an issue for New York agriculturalists — and it's getting worse.

> "We are seeing rainstorms that have a much longer and more fierce dumping of water." — Tim Terry

A major challenge for dairy farmers is controlling runoff from increasing rainfall and preventing contaminated water from entering local water bodies.

## Tackling Runoff at Mid-Knight Dairy

While working on the Dairy CAMF project, Tim focused on runoff at Mid-Knight Dairy. A creek divides the farm, running directly through its middle. The creek's flow can become violent during rainstorms. To improve resiliency, Tim worked with the farm to resize culverts — enhancing the creek's flow capacity while preventing clogs that would cause debris buildup, ponding, water damage, and runoff challenges.

![Holstein cows in the barn at Mid-Knight Dairy](/images/farmer-stories/tim-terry-2.jpg)

## A Holistic View of Agricultural Climate Change

As Tim reflected on the future of the dairy industry, he emphasized the importance of thinking about climate change in agriculture in a holistic sense — the entire agricultural enterprise needs to be accounted for, because what takes place in one part of the system influences all other parts.

> "By doing things a little differently in one part, it's going to make life a whole lot easier in another part." — Tim Terry
