Wife and husband duo **Jennifer** and **Chris Koval** operate a dairy farm milking 500 cows in Saratoga County, New York. The couple participated in the 2024 Dairy Climate Adaptation and Mitigation Fellowship (Dairy CAMF). The Kovals had a unique partnership: Jennifer served as the advisor fellow for her husband, Chris, the farmer fellow.

> "We've got to talk about goals and work through challenges together, daily." — Chris Koval

Jennifer works part-time supporting farmers at Cornell Cooperative Extension in Saratoga County, while Chris runs their dairy farm with his brother Adam. Their son Jack has recently joined the operation full-time. Jennifer reasoned that the only way she could get her husband to join Dairy CAMF was if she could play his other half as an advisor. During the program, she got to wear two hats — serving as Chris's agricultural advisor while also learning as a dairy farmer herself.

Her role as an advisor was not only helpful to her own farm but also influential in her work with Cooperative Extension. Jennifer shared that Dairy CAMF enabled her to better support farmers with the climate issues facing the agricultural community she is a part of.

## The Boomerang Effect: Too Wet, Then Too Dry

At the Koval Dairy, most of the challenges they face relate to extreme weather events. In the spring of 2025, they had difficulty planting corn on time because of muddy conditions. Then in early July, they received no rain at all for two weeks.

> "We've had sort of a boomerang effect, too wet then and now too dry." — Jennifer Koval

Dry periods coupled with significant heat have been a consistent concern. Periods of intense heat have caused significant stress on the cattle.

> "With all the fluctuations in heat and rain this summer, it's a little nerve-racking watching the weather." — Jennifer Koval

## Building Resilience Together

As a dynamic team, Jennifer and Chris worked together to find solutions.

> "We brainstormed different ways to build resilience into the farm. I mostly thought about the farmstead while Chris thought about cow comfort." — Jennifer Koval

Together, they found ways to keep their farm cool during heat waves — increasing shade cover by planting trees near barns and installing more energy-efficient fans to keep their cattle comfortable. For water management, they plan to build a water catchment system and a retention pond to be better equipped to handle both wet and dry conditions.

![Cattle on the Koval dairy farm in Saratoga County](/images/farmer-stories/jennifer-koval-2.jpg)

## Learning From Other Farmers

Because Chris was busy operating the farm, Jennifer handled the Dairy CAMF activities like the workbook and planning homework. She said that many of the plans were already in Chris's head, and she helped write them down. Both found value in connecting with other program participants.

> "I enjoyed the people in Dairy CAMF, because the conversations are what really lead to changes." — Chris Koval
