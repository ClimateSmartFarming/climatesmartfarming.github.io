The New York State Climate Impacts Assessment is a comprehensive, science-based investigation into how climate change is already affecting — and will continue to affect — communities, ecosystems, and the agricultural sector across New York State.
What It Covers
The assessment examines climate change impacts across multiple sectors including agriculture, water resources, ecosystems, and public health. For farmers, the agriculture chapter provides detailed analysis of:

Projected temperature and precipitation changes by region
Impacts on major commodity crops including dairy, vegetables, grapes, and field crops
Adaptation strategies being implemented by New York farmers
Recommendations for policy and program development

Why It Matters for Farmers
Cornell researchers Dr. Deborah Aller and Dr. Allison Chatrchyan led the Agriculture chapter of this assessment. Their work documents the real-world experiences of New York farmers already adapting to a changing climate, and lays out the science behind what to expect in coming decades.
Understanding these projections can help farmers make better long-term decisions about infrastructure investment, crop selection, water management, and business planning.
