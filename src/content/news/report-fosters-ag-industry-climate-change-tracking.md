![Climate Indicators for Agriculture report](/images/news/Screen-Shot-2021-02-09-at-6.04.23-PM.png#left) Art DeGaetano, professor of earth and atmospheric sciences at Cornell, is one of nine scientists who co-authored a report to help the nation's farmers, producers, and commercial agricultural managers reduce risk in the face of climate change. "We present a foundational report on how to keep the pulse on climate change in agriculture, what climate change indicators to watch, and how the indicators may change," said DeGaetano, who is also a fellow in the Cornell Atkinson Center for Sustainability.

The report, "Climate Indicators for Agriculture," was released July 29, 2020 by the U.S. Department of Agriculture. Since agriculture is sensitive to temperatures, precipitation, frost timing, pests, and many other factors, climate change significantly influences agricultural productivity and management decisions.

The report identifies 20 statistical indicators of climate change in five categories that help agricultural professionals address regional needs. Physical indicators include climate variables such as extreme precipitation, soil moisture, nighttime air temperature, heat waves, and humidity. Crop and livestock indicators measure factors such as animal heat stress and the ability to grow new crops in regions. Biological indicators track the range and infestation intensity of weeds, pests, and disease vectors. Phenological indicators cover seasonal activities affected by climate conditions, such as flowering and pollination dates, timing of budbreak in fruit trees, and insect generations per season. Socioeconomic indicators address human and economic factors such as crop insurance payments, net productivity, and heat-related mortality of agricultural workers.

"The report was intended to appeal to a wide variety of audiences," DeGaetano said. "We wanted to pick the indicators that were relevant to different agricultural sectors and sections around the country."

Co-authors included researchers from Colorado State University, the National Center for Atmospheric Research, Purdue University, Iowa State University, and the USDA.
