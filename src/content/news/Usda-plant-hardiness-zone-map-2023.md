Farmers, gardeners, and researchers take note! On November 15th, the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) released a new version of its Plant Hardiness Zone Map (PHZM) — updating this valuable tool for the first time since 2012. Cornell's Climate Smart Farming Program will link to this new map and incorporate it into Climate Smart Farming programming with agricultural producers.

The USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map is the standard by which gardeners and growers can determine which plants are most likely to thrive at a location. The new map — jointly developed by USDA's Agricultural Research Service (ARS) and Oregon State University's PRISM Climate Group — is more accurate and contains greater detail than prior versions. It is available online at [planthardiness.ars.usda.gov](https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/). The updated website also includes a new "Tips for Growers" section with information about USDA ARS research programs of interest to gardeners and plant breeders.

## What's New in the 2023 Map

The 2023 map is based on 30-year averages of the lowest annual winter temperatures at specific locations, divided into 10-degree Fahrenheit zones and further subdivided into 5-degree Fahrenheit half-zones. It incorporates data from 13,412 weather stations — compared to the 7,983 used for the 2012 map — and features greatly improved resolution for Alaska. When compared to the 2012 map, approximately half of the country shifted to the next warmer half-zone, reflecting temperature increases in the range of 0–5 degrees Fahrenheit over the period.

The map uses temperature data measured at weather stations from 1991 to 2020. Map developers caution against attributing zone shifts solely to global climate change, as the extreme minimum temperature is highly variable year to year and the improvements also reflect more sophisticated mapping methods and a larger station network.

As with the 2012 map, the 2023 version has 13 zones across the United States and its territories. The map is available as a free download for anyone who wishes to print copies.
