![Winter farm in New York](/images/news/winterfarm-ny.jpeg#left) The Cornell Climate Smart Farming Program hosted a series of winter webinars in early 2021 for farmers, Extension specialists, and agriculture consultants.

## Implications of Large Solar Installations and Leasing on Farmland

David Kay (Cornell) and Matt Johnson (NYS Tug Hill Commission) discussed the implications of solar siting on farmland and how to support renewable energy development while protecting farmland. Utility-scale solar projects have been expanding in New York due to the need for additional energy development, changing technology, and changes in policies at the state level.

## Using the CSF Growing Degree Day Calculator for Cropping Decisions

Kitty O'Neil (NNY Regional Ag Team) and Brian Belcher (Cornell) provided an in-depth look into the Growing Degree Day Tool, which calculates heat accumulation to predict crop plant, pest, and pathogen development for any field selected from a map. The webinar covered how Extension specialists and farmers use the tool to make decisions to optimize varieties and yields and minimize labor costs.

## Irrigation Management in Processing Vegetables

Elizabeth Buck, Steve Reiners, Julie Kickert, and Brian Belcher (Cornell University) presented on using the Water Deficit Calculator for irrigation management in processing vegetables. The webinar covered the physiology of drought stress in critical stages, how the tool works in detail, and examples of crops grown with irrigation or rain-fed fields.

## Additional Winter Sessions

The 2021 Long Island Agriculture Forum featured two sessions covering climate change, including the Beginner Farmer Session and the General Session on practices and tools to increase farm resiliency.

Dr. Jennifer Phillips of Gansvoort Farm in Clermont, NY presented on "Understanding Climate Change and its Impacts on Local Agriculture," covering what climate change means for growers in the Hudson Valley and how she is working to be more climate-resilient on her own farm.
