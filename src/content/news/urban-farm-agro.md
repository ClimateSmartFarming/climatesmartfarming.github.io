Agroforestry in New York City? It may be hard to imagine urban farms using a range of practices that integrate trees, forests, and agricultural production, but interest in and use of the practice is growing. In New York, agroforestry could look like food forests planted within a public park, or mushroom production integrated into a community garden.

In response to increasing interest in agroforestry in recent years, Cornell Cooperative Extension's Harvest New York Program, in collaboration with Cornell Small Farms, hosted its first annual NYC Urban Agroforestry Summit on July 14th, 2026, at Solar One. Over 100 participants gathered to learn and network with urban agroforestry practitioners, service providers, and city government partners on ways to scale agroforestry in NYC.

The event began with speakers from the Syracuse Food Forest Project, NYC Parks, Bronx River Alliance, the MYA Group, and Samuel's Food Gardens, as well as the NYC Office of Urban Agriculture and the Office of Climate and Environmental Justice, who discussed the evolution, current landscape, and future potential of agroforestry within New York City's food systems and urban forestry initiatives.

After lunch, participants connected with fellow practitioners, researchers, and advocates during a dynamic tabling and poster session to spark collaboration and resource sharing. One of the posters featured Urban CAMP participant Chase Louden, who presented the farm adaptation plan he developed for the Los Sures Food Forest project. Participants also joined facilitated small-group discussions to help shape research priorities and guide the strategic expansion of urban agroforestry across NYC, which will result in a report that will be shared with summit participants and policymakers.
