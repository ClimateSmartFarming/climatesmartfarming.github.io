Urban agriculture provides more than just fresh food to cities. Farms and garden spaces reduce the urban heat island effect, decrease stormwater runoff, provide wildlife habitat, and give city occupants a beautiful place to socialize and recreate. Recently, more frequent and intense flooding, drought, and record-breaking temperature events have negatively impacted urban farmers. To remain productive and profitable, urban farmers need support to address increasing weather extremes.

## Introducing Urban CAMP

In response to the challenges facing urban agriculture, the USDA Northeast Climate Hub, Cornell University, and Harvest NY Cooperative Extension developed the Urban Climate Adaptation and Mitigation Program (Urban CAMP) in New York City. NYC, the largest city in the U.S., was selected to pilot Urban CAMP because of its established urban farming network. The city is home to more than 2,000 registered or permitted community gardens, offering a diverse group of needs, strengths, and production models.

The program provides evidence-based information and recommendations to farmers and agricultural advisors to increase urban farm resilience. Lectures, group activities, and networking events build participant understanding of resilience and adaptation. Participants are also expected to do outreach, transferring their knowledge to others in the group and to the larger urban farming community.

> "The Urban CAMP program is excellent and much needed […] I intend to integrate climate adaptation and mitigation into my conversations with beginning farmers, and into my recommendations for improving Farm Service Agency offerings for urban agriculture!" — Urban CAMP Participant, 2025

## The 2025 Cohort

Approximately 30 participants attended the first session in February 2025, which highlighted variable weather pattern impacts and addressed the science behind why patterns are changing.

> "I gained a better understanding of the impact of climate change on urban agriculture and the measures that small scale growers can take to adapt and mitigate that impact." — Urban CAMP Participant, 2025

Between 20–35 people participated in subsequent sessions, with a mix of original attendees and new participants. All sessions focused on connecting with peers and building capacity through group activities. The five workshop sessions covered:

1. Climate Science and Impacts (February 26th)
2. Climate Adaptation on Urban Farms (March 5th)
3. Climate Mitigation on Urban Farms (March 12th)
4. Climate and Resiliency Planning (March 19th)
5. Finding Funding for Climate Resilience (March 26th)

> "Understanding how to take concepts learned throughout Urban CAMP and apply them to creating a plan for the farm, talking to other farms in the area and learning from their experience was very helpful!" — Urban CAMP Participant, 2025

## Fellows and Field Days

![The Urban CAMP cohort on their urban farm tour, June 21st 2025](/images/news/urban-camp-2.jpg)

In addition to the five workshop sessions, Urban CAMP also offers in-person field days, online webinars, and networking opportunities. Participants who complete the full program have been given the opportunity to become program fellows — attending field days, continuing to connect as a cohort, and developing adaptation plans for farms they work or volunteer on.

Adaptation plans will address each farm's unique vulnerabilities and select techniques to implement throughout the year. In December 2025, the program will wrap up with a closing event where fellows will present their adaptation plans and highlight opportunities they've had to share their knowledge with their communities.
